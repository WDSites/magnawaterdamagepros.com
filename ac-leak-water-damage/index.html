How Common Are AC Leaks In Magna

In the dry Salt Lake Valley climate, many older homes use evaporative coolers, often called swamp coolers, while newer ones use central air. Central units create condensation that drains through a small line, and that line can clog with algae. Evaporative coolers carry water by design, so their pads, lines and floats can leak into the roof or ceiling.

Evaporative coolers draw on household water supplied through the Magna Water District, which has served the area since 1949. Neither problem is as frequent as a burst pipe, but they show up every summer. We handle the water damage and point out where the leak came from, and an HVAC technician handles the equipment.

What An AC Leak Looks Like

Ceiling stain under an air handler

A brown patch below an attic or closet unit often points to a blocked condensate line.

A wet closet floor

A pan overflowing at a furnace closet can soak carpet and the wall behind it.

Rust trails on a wall

Wall-mounted units can drip behind the housing and leave streaks.

Damp roof curb or ceiling

An evaporative cooler can leak at the roof opening and soak the ceiling below.

First-Hour Safety Steps

  • Turn the system off at the thermostat and the breaker, because water and electrical parts are a hazard.
  • Catch drips with a bucket and lay plastic over furniture.
  • Do not touch wiring or the unit if water is dripping onto it.
  • Turn off the water supply line to an evaporative cooler.
  • Photograph the stain, the unit and the drain line.
  • Call an HVAC technician for the equipment and the dispatch line for drying.

How We Dry An AC Leak

Cooling leaks are usually clean water, but they often run through insulation and ceiling drywall. The crew uses meters to follow the spread, extracts what it can reach and removes soaked board and insulation. Drying equipment then runs until readings match a dry reference.

Step 01

Trace

Meters and a thermal camera follow the water from the unit to where it stopped.

Step 02

Contain

Drips are caught and furniture is protected.

Step 03

Remove wet material

Soaked insulation and ceiling board are cut out.

Step 04

Dry and log

Fans and dehumidifiers run while each visit adds a reading to the log.

A cooling leak is dried with ANSI/IICRC S500 in mind as a reference, and one that sat for weeks may also need mold remediation.

Summer Heat And Dry Air In The Valley

NOAA records show Salt Lake City is dry by national standards, at roughly 15.5 inches of precipitation yearly. Dry air speeds the evaporation of surface water, but it does not dry the inside of a ceiling cavity, so a stain that looks gone can still be wet.

Houses from the 1970s building wave often carry older cooling equipment with long drain runs. A yearly check of pans and lines in spring is a sensible habit.

Checking The Condensate Line

The condensate drain on a central unit is a small pipe that carries water outside or to a drain. A float switch on many units shuts the system off if the line clogs, but not all homes have one. A technician can clear the line and test the pan.

A simple habit is to look at the end of the drain line in spring and again in midsummer. If it is not dripping while the unit runs on a warm day, the line may be blocked.

What Changes The Cost

  • How long the unit leaked before anyone noticed.
  • Ceiling and insulation area that has to be opened.
  • Whether the water reached the attic floor or walls.
  • Drying days and equipment.
  • Painting and drywall repair after drying.

Insurance And Maintenance Leaks

Insurers often distinguish between a sudden failure and damage from lack of maintenance, and the Utah Insurance Department lists flood as excluded under homeowners policies, which rarely applies to a cooling leak. A clogged drain that overflowed might be treated differently from a pipe that burst. Read your policy, keep records of any service visits and photograph the unit before it is moved.

Mistakes To Avoid

  • Ignoring a stain that returns every time the unit runs.
  • Pouring bleach into a drain line without asking a technician.
  • Painting a ceiling before it has dried.
  • Leaving a wet attic floor to dry on its own.
