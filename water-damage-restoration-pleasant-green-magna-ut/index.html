Pleasant Green, Magna

Where Pleasant Green Is

Pleasant Green is one of the two farming settlements that merged into Magna when the copper industry arrived. Farmers settled it in the 1860s, and the name is still used today. Pleasant Green School stands at 8201 W 2700 S, and Pleasant Green Cemetery was established in 1883 near 3500 South.

No official boundary was found for the neighborhood, so this page describes it approximately as the area around 2700 South and 8200 to 8400 West on the west side of Magna.

What Is Verified About Water Here

A December 2008 Salt Lake County Flood Control master plan for Coon and Harkers Creeks describes how storm water moves through west Magna. It lists no dated flood events, and its flow figures come from modeled design storms.

The plan does flag a few trouble spots. One is a detention area at about 8300 West and 3650 South that reportedly causes basement flooding during high storm flows. Another is an older storm drain between 3100 and 3500 South, described as 50 to 60 years old in 2008. Current conditions and later upgrades were not verified, and these spots do not mean every home nearby is affected.

Basement And Ground-Floor Water

Basement data for Pleasant Green was not found, and the citywide median year built is 1979. If your home has a basement or low floor, water from a failed pipe, water heater or sump pump behaves the same way as anywhere else. It spreads under walls, wicks into drywall and settles in carpet pad.

Rising water from heavy rain is a different loss from a plumbing failure. Read storm water damage and flood damage restoration for how the two are handled.

Safety First When Water Appears

  • Keep people and pets out of standing water, which may hide live electrical hazards.
  • Shut off the main water valve only if it is dry to reach.
  • Do not run electrical equipment or switch breakers while standing in water.
  • Do not treat sewage or contaminated floodwater with fans or a household vacuum.
  • Photograph the damage when it is safe, and lift unaffected items off the floor.
  • Call (385) 988-9410 for extensive damage, and call your utility or emergency services for immediate danger.

How Extraction And Drying Work

A crew pumps or extracts the standing water, removes wet material that cannot be saved and sets air movers and dehumidifiers. Readings are taken again until walls and floors dry out to normal levels, and ANSI/IICRC S500 serves only as a reference.

Sewage-contaminated water needs containment and removal of porous materials, as covered on sewage backup cleanup. For the drying stage, see structural drying. For pumping, see emergency water removal.

Insurance And Local Resources

Photos and moisture records help describe the loss. Coverage depends on the cause and your policy, and the Utah Insurance Department notes that homeowners policies generally exclude flood, with flood policies having a waiting period of about 30 days.

FEMA publishes flood maps at msc.fema.gov, and Utah offers floodhazards.utah.gov. A mapped zone for a single address must be checked at the property level. Salt Lake County Flood Control gives out sandbags at 604 W 6960 S in Midvale, and sandbags do not seal out water.
