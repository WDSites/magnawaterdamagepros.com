A City That Grew Fast

West Jordan was incorporated on January 10, 1941. Its population rose from 4,221 in 1970 to 103,712 in 2010, and the 2020 census counted 116,961. That means most homes are less than 50 years old, with newer subdivisions toward the foothills.

Gardner Village, a shopping district in renovated historic mills and houses, dates back to an 1850 sawmill built by Archibald Gardner. Jordan Landing and the South Valley Regional Airport sit in other parts of the city. The Jordan River forms the eastern edge, and the Kennecott mine sits near the western edge.

Newer homes carry newer problems. Water heaters, supply lines and roof flashing from the 1990s and 2000s are reaching the age where failures cluster, and new construction can hide poor sealing around windows and doors.

Where Leaks Start In A Newer West Jordan House

Many West Jordan homes sit on slab or finished-basement foundations built in the last few decades. The usual starting points are the supply line behind a toilet, the hose to a washing machine, the ice maker line and the water heater, along with a roof valley or window flashing that was never sealed well. Water from these sources often runs inside a wall or under flooring for days before a stain appears.

A moisture meter and a thermal camera locate the wet area without opening every wall. The crew then removes only what cannot dry, such as saturated insulation or baseboard, and sets drying equipment to bring the structure back to normal readings. Daily visits record the numbers until the readings match a dry reference.
