What Storm Water Damage Means

Storm water damage covers water that enters a building because of weather: wind that tears shingles, hail that cracks a skylight, a downspout that dumps beside a foundation or snowmelt that finds a seam. It differs from flood damage, where rising outside water floods a lower level, though a bad storm can cause both.

The common thread is that the weather made an opening. After the storm passes, the opening and the wet materials remain, and water can keep moving into walls for days.

Where Storm Water Gets In

Roof openings

Missing shingles, damaged flashing and broken vents let rain reach the attic.

Windows and doors

Wind-driven rain can push past worn seals and soak the sill and wall below.

Window wells and grading

Heavy rain can overflow a well and send water into a basement.

Gutters and downspouts

Clogged or loose gutters spill water against the foundation.

Safety Steps During And After A Storm

  • Stay inside until lightning and high wind pass, and avoid damaged roofs and downed power lines.
  • Keep away from standing water if any outlet or cord is in it.
  • Cover roof openings with a tarp only if you can do it safely from the ground, or leave it to a roofer.
  • Move belongings out of wet rooms and photograph the damage before you touch it.
  • Switch off power to a wet area only if the panel is dry and reachable.
  • Call the dispatch line and a roofer for the opening.

How The Cleanup Works

Crews extract standing water, then use meters to find what is wet under paint and flooring. Wet insulation, ceiling board and carpet are removed when they cannot be dried, and air movers and dehumidifiers dry the rest. If storm water carried dirt or sewage into a space, sanitizing is added.

Step 01

Assess

Meters locate wet ceilings, walls and floors.

Step 02

Extract

Water is pumped or vacuumed out of the affected rooms.

Step 03

Remove

Soaked insulation, board and carpet come out.

Step 04

Dry and check

Dehumidifiers and air movers run until readings return to normal.

Snowmelt, Sandbags And Flood Maps In Magna

Salt Lake County Flood Control offers sandbags in runoff season and warns that they will not seal out water. They help steer it, not stop it.

You can check your flood zone by address at the FEMA Flood Map Service Center, msc.fema.gov, or the Utah Risk Map at floodhazards.utah.gov. Annual precipitation in Salt Lake City runs near 15.5 inches according to NOAA climate normals, so one hard storm matters more than the average suggests.

Water That Arrives Days Later

Storm water damage does not always show up during the storm. Snow that melts on a roof over several days can send water into the attic long after the sky has cleared, and saturated soil can push groundwater through a foundation crack well after the rain ends.

Check ceilings, window sills and basement corners for several days after a storm. A new stain, a damp smell or a bubble in the paint are reasons to call before the damage spreads.

Insurance Fit

Wind and hail damage is usually handled differently from flood. The Utah Insurance Department notes that homeowners policies exclude flood and that flood coverage typically has a waiting period of about 30 days. Photograph storm damage, keep receipts for emergency protection and report promptly.

Mistakes To Avoid

  • Climbing onto a wet roof to inspect damage.
  • Waiting for sunshine to dry a soaked wall.
  • Throwing away damaged items before photographing them.
  • Assuming a roof patch ends the water problem inside.
