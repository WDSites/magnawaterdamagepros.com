What Water Does To Carpet

Carpet has three layers: the fibers you see, the backing and the pad underneath. Water passes through the first two and settles in the pad, which acts like a sponge. Pad that stays wet loses its structure and can hold moisture against the subfloor for days.

The first question is the category of the water. Clean water from a supply line gives carpet a decent chance of being saved, while gray or sewage water usually means removal.

Safety Steps For The First Hour

  • Stop the water at its source and close the main valve if needed.
  • Avoid walking on soaked carpet, which pushes water into the pad and subfloor.
  • Turn off power to the room if outlets or cords are in the water.
  • Move furniture off the carpet and set foil or wood blocks under legs.
  • Do not use a household vacuum on wet carpet.
  • Photograph the area and call the dispatch line.

How Wet Carpet Is Dried

Extraction comes first, using a truck-mounted or portable extractor that pulls water from the fibers and pad. If the pad is saturated, it is lifted and removed, and the carpet is propped up so air can reach the underside. Air movers and dehumidifiers then dry the carpet, and meters check the subfloor.

Step 01

Test

The crew identifies the water category and tests the pad and subfloor.

Step 02

Extract

Water is pulled from the carpet and pad.

Step 03

Lift or remove

Pad is removed if wet, and carpet is lifted or floated for drying.

Step 04

Dry and confirm

Equipment runs until readings show the subfloor is dry.

Carpet decisions lean on ANSI/IICRC S500 as a reference, and your crew can explain when it advises replacement.

Basements, Winters And Older Homes

Many Magna homes from the 1970s have carpeted basements. Water in a basement often sits on concrete, so the carpet and the slab both need to dry.

Ground shaking can open small gaps in a basement slab, and Magna had a magnitude 5.7 earthquake in 2020. Owners who saw new cracks near a wall or slab should check carpeted rooms for dampness after a rain.

Odors After A Wet Carpet

A sour smell after carpet gets wet usually means the pad or subfloor is still damp. Surface deodorizers cover it for a day or two, but the smell returns until the moisture is gone. The fix is more drying, not more fragrance.

If the carpet is saved, a final cleaning and a deodorizing step come after the readings reach normal. Doing them earlier locks moisture into fibers that have not finished drying.

What Changes The Cost

  • Water category and whether carpet can be saved.
  • The square footage of carpet and pad affected.
  • Whether wet pad has to be replaced.
  • Drying time and equipment.
  • Replacing carpet and pad afterward.

Insurance Fit

Coverage follows the cause of the water. A basement flooded by groundwater falls under flood, which the Utah Insurance Department says homeowners policies exclude, while a burst supply line is a different question. Photograph the carpet before anything is removed and keep a sample if your adjuster asks.

Mistakes To Avoid

  • Laying heavy furniture back on damp carpet.
  • Letting a wet pad dry in place for weeks.
  • Steam cleaning carpet before it is dry.
  • Keeping carpet that sat in sewage water.

Standing water is first handled by emergency water removal, and the drying plan is described under structural drying.
