When a drain backs up

What A Sewage Backup Is

A sewage backup happens when wastewater flows back up through floor drains, tubs or toilets instead of out to the main. Causes include a blocked lateral line, roots in the pipe, a collapsed section and a surcharge in the public sewer. The water is classed as Category 3, which means it can carry bacteria and other contaminants.

That classification changes the whole job. Porous materials that touched the water are usually removed rather than dried, and hard surfaces are cleaned and sanitized.

Warning Signs

Slow drains in several fixtures

When the tub, sink and toilet all drain slowly, the main line may be restricted.

Gurgling toilets

Air pushed back through traps is a common first sign of a blockage.

Water in the lowest floor drain

Basement floor drains are the first place a backup appears.

Foul smell near drains

A sewer odor with no visible water can mean a trap has been broken.

Safety Steps Before Cleanup

  • Stop using sinks, tubs, washers and toilets so no more water enters the line.
  • Keep children, pets and anyone with health concerns away from the area.
  • Do not wade through the water, and stay out of rooms with wet outlets.
  • Turn off power to the affected area only if the panel is dry and safe to reach.
  • Do not use a household vacuum on sewage.
  • Call the dispatch line, then a plumber to clear the line.

How Sewage Cleanup Works

The crew sets up containment, wears protective equipment and extracts the contaminated water. Carpet, pad, insulation and soft drywall that touched it are removed and bagged. Hard surfaces are scrubbed and treated with an antimicrobial cleaner, and drying equipment finishes the job.

Step 01

Contain

Barriers limit the spread of contaminated air and water.

Step 02

Extract

Pumps and vacuums remove the liquid.

Step 03

Remove porous material

Carpet, pad and wet board are cut out and bagged.

Step 04

Sanitize and dry

Surfaces are cleaned, then equipment dries the structure.

ANSI/IICRC S500 is the reference for handling Category 3 water. Your crew should explain what is removed and why before work starts.

Health And Cleanup Limits

Sewage water can carry bacteria, viruses and parasites, which is why it is treated as the most serious water category. Skin contact, splashing and airborne droplets are all concerns, so crews wear gloves, boots, eye protection and respirators. Children, older adults and anyone with a weakened immune system should stay away from the house until the area is cleaned.

Items that cannot be cleaned, such as upholstered furniture, mattresses and stuffed toys that sat in the water, are typically discarded. Photograph them first so your insurer has a record of what was lost.

Insurance Fit

Sewage backup is often excluded from standard homeowners coverage unless a water backup endorsement is attached. Utah's insurance department says homeowners policies exclude flood, and backup is a separate question. Ask your insurer before work begins, and photograph everything before removal.

Mistakes To Avoid

  • Trying to mop and bleach a sewage spill without protective gear.
  • Running the washer or dishwasher while the line is blocked.
  • Keeping carpet that sat in sewage water.
  • Skipping the plumber, which lets the backup return.
