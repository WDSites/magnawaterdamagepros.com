Historic Downtown Magna

Where Historic Downtown Magna Sits

Historic Downtown Magna is the commercial core along West Main Street. The National Park Service lists the Magna Commercial Downtown Historic District, with 37 contributing buildings, on the National Register of Historic Places as of January 4, 2021. The Empress Theatre has its own listing, dated May 9, 1985.

The edges of the area are not officially defined for residential use, so this page describes it loosely as West Main Street and the older blocks around it. Early neighborhoods such as Greektown, Japantown, Snaketown and Little Italy grew around Main Street, according to the Magna history on Wikipedia. Those names are historical and are not used here as service boundaries.

What The Buildings Are Like

Near Main Street, real estate guides describe early 1900s bungalows and ranch-style homes, some of which need renovation. The citywide median year built is 1979, so the area around Main Street is older than the typical Magna house.

Age alone does not say how often a building floods or leaks. It does mean roofs, plumbing and wall finishes may have been repaired several times, so a stain on a ceiling can sit far from the real source. Neighborhood-specific data on basements and crawl spaces was not verified for this page.

Water Problems Worth Checking First

Roof and flashing leaks

Rain or snowmelt can run along old framing before it stains plaster or board. See roof leak water damage.

Aging supply and drain lines

Original pipes and fittings in older buildings can fail at joints. See frozen pipe damage for cold-weather breaks.

Shared walls and floors

A leak in one unit of a mixed-use block can reach the shop or apartment next to it.

Sewer or drain backups

Contaminated water needs a crew, not a mop. See sewage backup cleanup.

Safety First When Water Appears

  • Stay out of standing water near outlets or a panel, and keep pets away from it.
  • Close the water supply if the valve sits on dry ground and turns easily.
  • Leave breakers alone and keep cords out of the wet area.
  • Take photos from a dry spot before anything gets moved.
  • Raise small valuables onto a table or shelf when that is safe.
  • Call (385) 988-9410 when the damage is widespread. Gas, fire or sparking wires call for the utility or 911 first.

Extraction And Drying In Older Structures

Extraction removes standing water first. Moisture meters and thermal imaging then show where water traveled, and air movers and dehumidifiers dry the materials that can be saved. Wet plaster, insulation or flooring that cannot dry in place is removed.

Brick and old framing hold moisture longer than new drywall, so readings are repeated until the materials read dry. The published ANSI/IICRC S500 standard is cited here only as a reference. For a full drying plan, see structural drying and dehumidification, or emergency water removal for the first pumping stage.

Insurance Documentation

Dated photos, moisture readings and drying notes give a clear record of what happened and what was done. Coverage turns on the cause of the loss and the wording of your policy, so ask your insurer before assuming anything. Water that rises from outside is normally treated as flood.

Business owners should check commercial coverage separately, and commercial water damage explains what a loss record usually includes.
